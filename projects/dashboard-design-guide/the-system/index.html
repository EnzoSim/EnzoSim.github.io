The system

The pages and figures are built from a small set of tokens and six decisions. They are listed here so the design can be rebuilt or adapted, with a model that moves three of them, every source the guide relies on, and a credit for every photograph.

In this chapter
  1. 6Six decisions
  2. 3Tokens you can move
  3. —Sources, editions and credits
The palette

Ground, sheet, field and well; slate for data and ink for what matters most. Cobalt stays outside the figures, for links and focus.

Six decisions

Each one is a rule from chapters 2 to 5, set as a value
Grid · Rule 1

The page: twelve columns of 64 px with 32 px gutters. The figures: all twelve columns, split into regions of whole columns, four and eight or six and six, inside sheets with an 8 px inset.

Surfaces · Rule 5

Ground, sheet, field and well. Higher is lighter. An inner radius is the outer radius minus the inset: 20 for a sheet, 12 for a field.

Type · Rule 9
294.4$K

One family. Values 48 px at weight 600, text and figures 15 px, labels 14 px, ticks 12 px. Tabular, duplexed figures.

Figures · Rule 12
−17.6against plan
57.0budget left

A bold value over a grey label at each mark. One decimal for money in thousands. A real minus sign. Plain words, such as budget left, with every term defined where it appears.

Colour · Rule 6

Greys only in the figures: slate for the main data, paler slate for secondary data, hatching for forecasts, a dashed outline for plans. Ink for what matters most and for selection. No red.

Interaction · Rule 11

Controls sit on the chart they change. Pointing highlights and never filters. Every value is printed, so nothing depends on hover.

1 · A grid that divides
5 sources
  • Gerstner, Designing Programmes, 1964The Capital grid of 1962: a page 58 units wide with 2-unit gutters, which carries one to six columns of whole units.
  • Few, With Dashboards, Formatting and Layout Definitely Matter, 2008Counts the rigid symmetrical grid among the layout mistakes of dashboard design.
  • Müller-Brockmann, Grid Systems in Graphic Design, 1981The standard manual of grid construction. It calls the grid an aid, not a guarantee.
  • Bosshard, The Typographic Grid, 2000About two dozen of the author’s own works with the grids behind them, and no single recipe.
  • Vignelli, National Park Service, Unigrid, 1977One sheet system for the brochures of more than 400 parks. The first brochure to use it appeared in 1978.
2 · Instruments
10 sources
  • Tufte, Sparkline theory and practice, from Beautiful Evidence, 2006Word-sized graphics that set a value beside its history, with a grey band for the normal range in the medical examples.
  • Few, Bullet Graph Design Specification, 2006, revised 2013The formal definition: a featured measure, a comparator and optional qualitative ranges.
  • Brinton, Graphic Methods for Presenting Facts, 1914An early American manual of charts for business, free on the Internet Archive and still a good source of component-bar forms.
  • Spear, Charting Statistics, 1952Her range bar, with an interquartile box, predates the box plot. A compact way to show spread within a row.
  • Hollifield, Oliver, Nimmo, Habibi, The High Performance HMI Handbook, 2008Grey screens, analog indicators with a normal range, and colour only for abnormal states.
  • ISA, ANSI/ISA-101.01-2015, human-machine interfaces for process automation systemsSets out a life cycle for process displays around each site’s written HMI philosophy and style guide. It does not itself prescribe grey screens; that comes from high-performance HMI practice.
  • Braseth and colleagues, Information Rich Design, HaldenLarge-screen process overviews built from aligned mini-trends and Gestalt grouping.
  • Desbarats, Why I stopped using bullet graphs, Nightingale, 2022Eleven objections. Problems do not stand out, and lower-is-better and ideal-range measures need variants. His alternative marks only what crosses an action threshold.
  • Chin, with Bryar, The Amazon Weekly Business Review, Commoncog, 2024Amazon’s weekly review of 400 to 500 metrics, most drawn as one chart: the trailing six weeks beside the trailing twelve months.
  • Powsner, Tufte, Graphical summary of patient status, The Lancet, 1994Small aligned histories with normal ranges on one page. A clinical ancestor of the sparkline.
3 · Routine variation
1 source
  • Wheeler, Understanding Variation, 1993Process behaviour charts separate routine weekly variation from a real change. A stronger comparator than last period once history exists.
4 · Drawn identities
3 sources
  • Playfair, The Commercial and Political Atlas, 1786Import and export lines with the area between them tinted as the balance. An early case of an accounting identity drawn as a shape.
  • Vicente, Rasmussen, Ecological interface design: theoretical foundations, IEEE SMC 1992Make the constraints of the work domain directly visible. The theory behind drawing an identity as geometry.
  • Bennett, Flach, Display and Interface Design: Subtle Science, Exact Art, 2011Configural displays and emergent features. When variables are drawn so their relationship forms a shape, a fault changes the shape.
5 · Surfaces and shading
12 sources
  • Imhof, through Tufte, Cartographic Relief Presentation, 1965; Envisioning Information, chapter 5Strong colour in small areas on a muted ground, and large areas kept quiet. The rule behind one accent on tonal surfaces.
  • Robinson, The Look of Maps, 1952Visual hierarchy and figure and ground in cartography. Maps solved layered surfaces long before interfaces did.
  • Patterson; Jenny, Shaded relief practice and researchHow Swiss-style relief generalises terrain and adjusts light locally. Shading as a selective, designed act.
  • Palmer, Common region: a new principle of perceptual grouping, Cognitive Psychology, 1992Elements inside a shared boundary group strongly and hierarchically. The basis for nested surfaces, and the reason every panel boundary is a claim.
  • Albers, Interaction of Color, 1963A colour is read relative to its surround.
  • Cleveland, McGill; Heer, Bostock, Graphical perception, JASA 1984; crowdsourced replication, CHI 2010Position on a common scale is read most accurately; shade and area least.
  • Szafir; Stone, Modeling color difference for visualization design, 2018; In color perception, size matters, 2012Small marks need larger colour differences to be told apart. Thin lines and 3 px bars cannot rely on subtle tints.
  • Harrison, Ishii, Vicente, Buxton, Transparent layered user interfaces, CHI 1995Measured how translucency trades focus against awareness. The evidence for and against see-through panels.
  • Colby, Scholl; Cooper, Transparency and blur as selective cues, 1991; Information Landscapes, 1994The MIT Visible Language Workshop treated translucency, blur and depth as information design. An origin of the soft layered look.
  • Google; Radix, Material 3 colour roles; Radix 12-step scalesTwo working role systems. Material names five container tones by emphasis. Radix gives each of twelve steps a job.
  • IBM, Carbon Design System, colour and layeringSurface roles as named tokens. In the light themes, layers alternate between white and a pale grey.
  • W3C, WCAG 2.2, non-text contrastThe parts of a control or a graphic that a reader must see need a contrast of at least three to one against what is next to them.
6 · Notation

  • Bertin, Semiology of Graphics, 1967Names the visual variables a mark can vary by, texture among them. The link is a summary.

8 · Tables
9 sources
  • Bartram, Correll, Tory, Untidy data: the unreasonable effectiveness of tables, IEEE TVCG 2022Data workers want to see and touch the table throughout analysis. Tables are a visualization idiom in their own right.
  • Ehrenberg, Rudiments of numeracy, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society A, 1977The classic paper on numeric tables: round hard, order rows by size, and put figures to be compared in columns.
  • Schwabish, Ten guidelines for better tables, Journal of Benefit-Cost Analysis, 2020A modern checklist: right-align numbers, show units sparingly, lighten the rules.
  • Tschichold; Rutter, Tschichold’s table rules, quoted by Rutter, 2017Set tables as text to be read, keep furniture to a minimum, avoid vertical rules.
  • Rao, Card, The Table Lens, CHI 1994A table whose cells become small bars, with rows that open to text on focus. Bertin’s in-cell bars, brought to the screen.
  • Gratzl, Lex, Gehlenborg, Pfister, Streit, LineUp, IEEE InfoVis 2013Ranks items on several weighted attributes with stacked bars inside a table.
  • Sutnar, Lönberg-Holm, Catalog Design Progress, 1950Product information designed as function, flow and form. The classic on structuring commercial data for fast lookup.
  • Crouwel, van de Wouw, Dutch telephone directory, 1977Condensed Univers, lower case only, numbers placed before names so no leader dots were needed.
  • Twyman, A schema for the study of graphic language, 1979Classifies graphic language by configuration, including list, matrix and branching. Useful vocabulary for deciding what a view is.

10 · Uncertainty
8 sources
  • Britton, Fisher, Whitley, The Inflation Report projections: understanding the fan chart, Bank of England, 1998The fan chart, first published in February 1996, shows a forecast as nested probability bands in place of a line with bounds.
  • Kay, Kola, Hullman, Munson, When (ish) is My Bus?, CHI 2016Introduced quantile dotplots: a distribution as about twenty countable dots. Readers estimated probabilities more consistently than from density curves.
  • Fernandes, Walls, Munson, Hullman, Kay, Uncertainty displays using quantile dotplots or CDFs improve transit decision-making, CHI 2018Decision quality, measured. Dotplots and cumulative curves beat intervals and point estimates.
  • Hullman, Resnick, Adar, Hypothetical outcome plots, PLOS ONE 2015Animated draws from a distribution outperformed error bars and violins for judging which of two quantities is larger.
  • Correll, Gleicher, Error bars considered harmful, IEEE TVCG 2014Bars with whiskers make values inside the bar look likelier than values outside it.
  • Joslyn, LeClerc, Uncertainty forecasts improve weather-related decisions, Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied, 2012Showing uncertainty improved decisions and softened the effect of a forecast that missed.
  • Padilla, Kay, Hullman, Uncertainty visualization, Wiley StatsRef, 2021The best single review: the techniques, the psychology behind each, and frequency framing.
  • Savage, The Flaw of Averages, 2009Plans built on average demand are wrong on average. An argument for planning against a distribution once one exists.

  • Victor, Up and Down the Ladder of Abstraction, 2011An interactive essay that moves between one concrete case and the whole range of a parameter.
  • Victor, Explorable Explanations, 2011Reactive documents: a number in a sentence can be dragged, and the text recomputes.
  • Bostock, Carter, Tse, Is It Better to Rent or Buy?, The New York Times, 2014Each slider sits on a small chart of the outcome across the whole range of that input.
  • Sedlmair, Heinzl, Bruckner, Piringer, Möller, Visual parameter space analysis, IEEE TVCG 2014A framework for exploring model inputs and outputs: a data flow, four navigation strategies and six tasks.
  • Howard, Decision analysis: practice and promise, Management Science, 1988Usually cited for the tornado diagram: one-way sensitivities sorted by swing, each input moved between assessed low and high values. A form for ranking assumptions by their effect on a result.
  • Fildes, Goodwin, Lawrence, Nikolopoulos, Effective forecasting and judgmental adjustments, International Journal of Forecasting, 2009Small overrides tended to hurt accuracy, large reasoned ones helped, and adjustments leaned optimistic.
  • Gilliland, Forecast value added: a reality check, Foresight, 2013Compare each step of the process with a naive forecast. The stairstep report shows where review adds or removes value.

Further reading · forecasting and retail practice
12 sources
  • Fildes, Ma, Kolassa, Retail forecasting: research and practice, International Journal of Forecasting, 2022The standard review of retail forecasting, from strategic decisions down to store and item.
  • Fisher, Hammond, Obermeyer, Raman, Making supply meet demand in an uncertain world, Harvard Business Review, 1994Sport Obermeyer, a skiwear maker, planned its buying around how uncertain each style’s demand was.
  • Kolassa, Rostami-Tabar, Siemsen, Demand Forecasting for Executives and Professionals, 2023A current, readable text with an online edition.

  • Syntetos, Boylan, Croston, On the categorization of demand patterns, Journal of the Operational Research Society, 2005Classes items as smooth, erratic, intermittent or lumpy from two statistics, with cut-offs at 1.32 and 0.49.
  • Wickramasuriya, Athanasopoulos, Hyndman, Optimal forecast reconciliation through trace minimization, JASA, 2019Forecasts at item, category and total level can be made to add up.
  • Makridakis, Spiliotis, Assimakopoulos, The M5 accuracy competition, International Journal of Forecasting, 202242,840 hierarchical Walmart series with many zeros. Evidence on what works for intermittent retail demand.
  • Ferreira, Lee, Simchi-Levi, Analytics for an online retailer, Manufacturing and Service Operations Management, 2016Demand forecasting and price optimisation at Rue La La, an online fashion retailer, with a measured revenue effect.
  • Caro, Gallien, Clearance pricing optimization for a fast-fashion retailer, Operations Research, 2012Zara’s markdown process, tested in a field experiment.
  • Operations research, The newsvendor critical ratioFor a single buy before a season, the best service level is the underage cost over the sum of underage and overage cost. A principled source for a stock target.
  • Vermorel, Lokad, Prioritized orderingRank every possible purchase unit by expected economic return and buy down the list until the budget is spent. An alternative to item-by-item alerts.
  • Forrester; Sterman, Industrial Dynamics, 1961; Business Dynamics, 2000Stock-and-flow notation. Inventory is a stock; sales and receipts are flows.
