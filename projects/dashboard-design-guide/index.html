A study guide in nine chapters · Enzo Simier · October 2026

Learn dashboard design with me

A planning dashboard has to show a chain of arithmetic: what demand asks for, what stock can serve, what is on order, how much budget is left to buy, and what margin results. The craft of drawing such chains is older than the screen.

This guide follows the people who worked it out, in grid design, cartography, control rooms, business reporting, perception research and interactive explanation. It shows who they were and what they made, turns their work into twelve rules with a model to play with for each, reads what neighbouring fields have since found, and opens the dashboards we built with those rules.

One plan runs through the guide

Five product lines, four weeks, sample data

A retailer plans four weeks of sales for five product lines. Demand for boots outruns stock in the week of 12 October, because an order of 240 pairs arrives a week too late. Receiving 80 of those pairs a week early would serve the demand. The plan tells that story in the order a planner works: four instruments, then sales and stock week by week, then the buying budget, then the decisions. Each decision has a switch, and every figure recomputes when one is turned on.

  • About the dataEvery figure is sample data and is computed in the page. There is no fitted forecast behind it, so the plan shows no forecast interval anywhere. Rule 10 explains why.
